The Reality

Running on fumes is no way to save dogs.

Dog rescues do extraordinary work, but most operate in a perpetual financial squeeze that leaves them one slow fundraising month away from crisis.

Donations disappear immediately

Vet bills, food, transport, foster supplies: every dollar that comes in goes right back out. There's no cushion, no plan, and no way to say no to a dog in need.

No long-term savings strategy

Most small rescues have never thought about holding reserves as a financial strategy. Bitcoin changes that calculus, but only if someone shows you how.

Bitcoin donors have nowhere to give

A growing segment of mission-driven donors holds Bitcoin. Right now, most rescues can't accept it, which means they're leaving donations on the table every month.

Why I started Bitcoin for Bullies

It started with a dog named Basia. My wife Carolyn and I adopted her from the Denver Animal Shelter, where she had been brought in as a stray: underweight, ribs showing, seven months old and already fighting for her life. She was a pit bull/boxer mix, the kind of dog that gets overlooked, misunderstood, and left waiting the longest. We brought her home, and she became the center of our world. She was the first dog Carolyn and I had together, and she changed us.

Later in life, Basia was diagnosed with a vestibular tumor. Most dogs with that diagnosis live only a few months. Basia gave us nearly a full year more. Every day she reminded us what resilience looks like. We loved her deeply, and losing her only strengthened our commitment to the dogs still waiting.

About two and a half years ago, we adopted Honey, a pit bull/lab mix, through A Friend of Jack rescue in Denver. Carolyn had followed Honey's story for years before we finally brought her home. Honey came to A Friend of Jack from a rescue in Roswell, New Mexico, where she had been found as part of a dog fighting operation. She was most likely used as a bait dog and to produce puppies for the organizers. She had every reason not to trust people. She trusts us completely. She's our girl, and she's a daily reminder of what these dogs are capable of when someone gives them a chance.

Bitcoin for Bullies was built on all of that: the love for these dogs, the frustration with how underfunded their rescues are, and a belief that Bitcoin could be a real financial tool for nonprofits doing the hard work. We fund partner rescues that pull pit bull-type dogs from shelters, and we help those rescues start accepting Bitcoin so they can reach a donor base that's largely been closed off to them. JMK Consulting grew directly out of that work.
